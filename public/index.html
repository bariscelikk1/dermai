DermAI sorts a dermoscopic image into one of seven diagnostic categories and reports how much of that probability sits in malignant classes. It is trained on the HAM10000 archive of 10,015 annotated lesions, and tuned not for headline accuracy but for melanoma recall, because in skin cancer, the two kinds of error do not cost the same.

Abstract

Summary of the work

Cutaneous melanoma is among the most treatable cancers when detected early and among the most lethal when detected late: five-year relative survival falls from over 99% for localized disease to roughly 35% once the disease is distant. That gradient makes early recognition a disproportionately high-leverage intervention. Dermoscopy improves diagnostic performance substantially over unaided visual inspection, but it is operator-dependent and requires training that is unevenly distributed across health systems.

This project develops a convolutional classifier over the seven diagnostic categories of the HAM10000 dermoscopic archive. An ImageNet-pretrained EfficientNet-B0 backbone is adapted using a two-stage linear-probing-then-fine-tuning (LP-FT) protocol, with all BatchNormalization layers held frozen throughout fine-tuning. The dataset's 58:1 class imbalance is addressed by bounded oversampling of the training split alone, after a lesion-level rather than image-level partition that eliminates near-duplicate leakage between training and validation. The system is evaluated with melanoma recall as the primary endpoint, on the argument that in a screening context a false negative and a false positive carry asymmetric cost.

Rationale

Why melanoma recall, and not accuracy

The HAM10000 archive is dominated by a single benign category: melanocytic nevi account for roughly two-thirds of all images. A classifier that predicted "benign mole" for every input would therefore report an accuracy figure that sounds respectable while detecting no cancer whatsoever. Accuracy, in this setting, is close to meaningless.

The metric that carries clinical meaning is recall on the melanoma class: of all true melanomas presented, what proportion did the system flag? A missed melanoma may progress through the survival gradient above. A false alarm, by contrast, costs an outpatient appointment and a period of anxiety. These are not commensurate outcomes, and the optimisation target reflects that.

A screening system that is wrong in the cautious direction remains useful. A screening system that is wrong in the reassuring direction is worse than no system at all, because it displaces the concern that would otherwise have sent someone to a clinic.

Research questions

What this project set out to answer

  1. RQ1

    Can a compact, single-GPU-trainable architecture reach clinically interesting melanoma recall on HAM10000?

    EfficientNet-B0 has ~5.3M parameters and trains within the session limits of a free Kaggle GPU notebook, making the work reproducible without institutional compute.

  2. RQ2

    Does the LP-FT protocol measurably protect pretrained features compared with naive end-to-end fine-tuning?

    Kumar et al. (ICLR 2022) show fine-tuning can distort pretrained features and underperform out-of-distribution; this project applies their two-stage remedy in a medical imaging setting.

  3. RQ3

    At an imbalance ratio of 58:1, does bounded oversampling outperform loss reweighting?

    Class weighting was implemented first and failed to converge; the substitution of bounded oversampling is documented as an empirical finding rather than a design preference.

  4. RQ4

    How much does lesion-level splitting change reported performance relative to image-level splitting?

    HAM10000 contains multiple images per physical lesion; the difference between the two protocols is a direct measure of leakage-inflated optimism.

Engineering problems

Four failure modes the pipeline had to survive

Each of the following was encountered during development rather than anticipated in advance. They are documented here because the solutions constitute the substantive engineering content of the project.

01

Data leakage between splits

HAM10000 records multiple photographs of the same physical lesion under distinct image_id values sharing one lesion_id. An image-level split places near-duplicates on both sides of the boundary, so the model is validated partly on images it has effectively memorised. The partition is therefore performed over unique lesion identifiers.

Resolved in data.py, lesion_level_split()

02

Extreme class imbalance

The majority class outnumbers the rarest, dermatofibroma (n = 115), by roughly 58 to 1. Loss reweighting through class_weight was attempted first and failed to converge, as the reweighted minority gradients dominated early updates. Bounded oversampling of the training split (raising each class to 50% of the majority count) converged stably.

Resolved in data.py, oversample()

03

Destruction of pretrained features

Fine-tuning a randomly initialised head jointly with the backbone propagates large early gradients into ImageNet weights that took far more data to learn than this dataset contains. Linear probing first, then fine-tuning at a tenth of the learning rate, preserves them. BatchNormalization layers stay frozen throughout.

Resolved in model.py, unfreeze_for_finetune()

04

Non-persistent training sessions

Training ran on Kaggle, where sessions terminate and working storage does not persist. Keras's stock checkpoint callbacks were additionally unusable due to a deepcopy incompatibility in the installed version. Checkpointing and learning-rate scheduling were therefore reimplemented to serialise both weights and scheduler state each epoch.

Resolved in callbacks.py

Contributions

What is claimed, and what is not

This project does

  • Implement a reproducible seven-class dermoscopic classifier trainable on freely available compute.
  • Apply the LP-FT protocol of Kumar et al. to a medical imaging task, with BatchNorm handling documented explicitly.
  • Report a lesion-level rather than image-level evaluation, avoiding a common source of inflated results in this dataset.
  • Treat melanoma recall as the primary endpoint and justify that choice epidemiologically.
  • Document negative results (specifically the failure of loss reweighting) rather than presenting only the successful path.

This project does not

  • Constitute a diagnostic device, or carry any regulatory clearance.
  • Validate on prospectively collected or Turkish patient data.
  • Establish performance across Fitzpatrick skin phototypes, which HAM10000 does not represent evenly.
  • Generalise to non-dermoscopic photography; the training distribution is contact dermoscopy under controlled illumination.
  • Claim superiority over, or equivalence to, examination by a qualified dermatologist.
