Epidemiology

Global disease burden

Skin cancers divide into cutaneous melanoma and the far more common keratinocyte carcinomas, grouped as non-melanoma skin cancer. Melanoma is the smaller category by incidence and the larger by lethality per case.

Category New cases, 2022 Deaths, 2022
Cutaneous melanomaArises from melanocytes; the target of this project's primary metric. 331,722 58,667
Non-melanoma skin cancerPredominantly basal cell and squamous cell carcinoma. ≈ 1,200,000 69,416

Source: GLOBOCAN 2022, International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC/WHO).

The comparison is instructive. Non-melanoma skin cancer produces more total deaths than melanoma despite being individually far less aggressive, simply because it is roughly four times as common. Both categories are represented in the classification scheme used here: basal cell carcinoma appears as bcc, and actinic keratoses and intraepithelial carcinoma (precursors on the squamous pathway) as akiec.

Modifiable aetiology

IARC attributes more than 80% of cutaneous melanoma worldwide to ultraviolet radiation exposure, approximately 267,000 of the roughly 332,000 cases recorded in 2022. This matters for a screening argument: the disease is both largely preventable and, when caught early, largely curable. The dominant failure mode is late presentation, not intractable biology.

Prognosis

The survival gradient

The single most important quantitative fact underlying this project is how sharply melanoma prognosis depends on stage at diagnosis. The United States SEER programme reports five-year relative survival by extent of disease as follows.

Localized

No spread beyond the skin of origin

>99%

Regional

Spread to nearby structures or lymph nodes

≈76%

Distant

Metastasis to lung, liver, brain or distant nodes

≈35%

Source: SEER programme figures as reported by the American Cancer Society.

A patient whose melanoma is identified while localized has a near-normal life expectancy with respect to that disease. The same disease identified after distant metastasis carries roughly a one-in-three five-year survival. The interval separating those two states is a window in which a diagnosis can be made, and it is precisely that window a screening aid is intended to widen.

This is the entire argument for optimising recall over accuracy. The cost of the two error types is separated by a factor visible in the survival table above.

National context

Disease burden in Türkiye

Turkish national cancer registry figures published by the Ministry of Health's General Directorate of Public Health record the following age-standardised incidence rates.

Category Men (per 100,000) Women (per 100,000)
Melanoma 1.9 1.3
Non-melanoma skin cancer 23.4 15.5

Source: T.C. Sağlık Bakanlığı, Halk Sağlığı Genel Müdürlüğü, Türkiye Kanser İstatistikleri.

Approximately 20,000 people receive a non-melanoma skin cancer diagnosis in Türkiye each year. Melanoma incidence is lower than in northern European or Australasian populations, which is consistent with the protective effect of higher average Fitzpatrick phototype, but lower incidence is not lower stakes. Populations with lower baseline risk often have correspondingly lower clinical suspicion, and later presentation is a recognised consequence.

A caution on trend figures

Figures circulating in the Turkish press describe a very large proportional rise in melanoma incidence over recent decades. Such increases are real in many registries but are confounded by improved case ascertainment and registry coverage, so a portion of any measured rise reflects better counting rather than more disease. This project does not rely on trend claims; the static incidence figures above, drawn directly from the national registry, are sufficient to motivate the work.

Modality

Dermoscopy, and why the input distribution matters

A dermatoscope is a handheld instrument combining magnification with polarised or immersion illumination. It renders subsurface structures, pigment networks, streaks, globules, blue-white veils, vascular patterns that are simply not visible to the unaided eye. Every image in the HAM10000 training set was acquired this way.

Comparison Finding

Sources: Kittler et al. meta-analysis; Reiter et al. systematic review and meta-analysis. See references.

Direct consequence for this system

Because the model has only ever seen dermoscopic images, an ordinary smartphone photograph of a mole is an out-of-distribution input. The network will still emit a confident-looking probability distribution (softmax always sums to one) but that confidence is not calibrated for an input type the model never encountered in training. This is the single most important practical limitation of the deployed tool, and it is stated again on the analysis page and in ethics.

Clinical criteria

The ABCDE criteria

The mnemonic used in clinical education and public health messaging to describe features raising suspicion of melanoma. It is included here because it is what the network is implicitly learning to approximate from pixels, and because it remains the practical guidance for anyone examining their own skin.

A

Asymmetry

One half of the lesion does not mirror the other.

B

Border

Edges are irregular, notched, scalloped or poorly defined.

C

Colour

Multiple shades (browns, blacks, reds, whites or blues) within one lesion.

D

Diameter

Larger than about 6 mm, though melanomas can be detected smaller.

E

Evolving

Change over time in size, shape, colour, elevation, or new symptoms such as bleeding or itching.

The E criterion deserves emphasis because it is the one no single-image classifier can assess. Evolution is a temporal signal; this model sees one frame. A lesion that is changing warrants examination regardless of what any static analysis reports.

Positioning

Where automated analysis fits

The evidence on machine classification of pigmented lesions is substantial and, on benchmark tasks, favourable. Esteva et al. trained a CNN on 129,450 clinical images spanning 2,032 diseases and reported performance comparable to 21 board-certified dermatologists. Tschandl et al. subsequently compared 511 human readers against 139 algorithms from 77 laboratories and found that the best machine classifiers outperformed human readers on the benchmark.

Two qualifications are essential, and both are reasons this project does not claim clinical utility. First, benchmark performance on curated archives has repeatedly failed to transfer cleanly to prospective clinical populations, where prevalence, image quality and case mix all differ. Second, the same authors' later work on human–computer collaboration found the productive configuration to be the clinician supported by the algorithm, not replaced by it.

The intended role

A tool of this kind is best understood as a triage prior: a cheap, instantaneous signal that may raise the probability someone presents for examination they would otherwise have deferred. It adds a step before the clinic. It removes none.
