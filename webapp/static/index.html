Important — not a medical device

DermAI is a university research prototype. It does not diagnose, and it must never be used to decide whether to seek care. The model was trained exclusively on dermoscopic images captured with a contact dermatoscope; ordinary phone photographs fall outside its training distribution and results on them are unreliable. Any concern about a mole or skin lesion belongs with a qualified dermatologist.

Methodology

How the model was built

Lesion-level splitting

HAM10000 contains several images of the same physical lesion. Splitting at image level would place near-duplicates on both sides of the train/validation boundary, inflating accuracy without improving real performance. The split is therefore performed over unique lesion_id values, at 85 / 15.

Class imbalance, 58 : 1

Minority classes are oversampled on the training split only, until each reaches 50% of the majority count; validation keeps the true distribution. class_weight was tried first and failed to converge — at 58 : 1 the reweighted gradients dominate early updates. Duplicated rows receive independent augmentation each epoch, so the effective inputs differ.

Two-stage LP-FT

Linear probing trains the classifier head against a frozen EfficientNetB0 backbone, so the randomly-initialised head cannot push large gradients into pretrained weights. Fine-tuning then unfreezes the backbone at a tenth of the learning rate.

Frozen BatchNormalization

During fine-tuning every BatchNormalization layer stays frozen. Left trainable, HAM10000's small oversampled batch statistics overwrite the ImageNet running statistics, which surfaces as a validation loss explosion within a few epochs.

Limitations

What this system cannot do

  • It is not a diagnosis. Output is a probability distribution from a research model, not a clinical finding.
  • Dermoscopic images only. Training data was captured through a contact dermatoscope under controlled lighting. Phone photographs are out of distribution and degrade accuracy substantially.
  • Population skew. HAM10000 is drawn largely from European populations and lighter skin phototypes; performance on darker skin is not validated here.
  • Headline accuracy is not the clinical metric. With 67% of the dataset in a single benign class, overall accuracy is a weak signal — melanoma recall is the number that matters, and a confident benign prediction never rules out cancer.
